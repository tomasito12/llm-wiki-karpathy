# Knowledge Management

## Executive synthesis

Knowledge management in AI is the practice of making information usable over time: creating, organizing, linking, sharing, and maintaining documents, policies, FAQs, summaries, and structured knowledge so models, agents, and people can retrieve and trust it later. The sources agree that this is not just storage; it is a living layer that improves answer quality, consistency, auditability, and reuse, especially when an assistant must remember across sessions or support ongoing operations. The main caveat is that the sources describe the concept more than they debate it, so this page gives a solid operational definition but not a full implementation guide.

## What to remember

- It is about usable knowledge, not just stored files.
- The key functions are creating, organizing, linking, sharing, and updating information.
- In AI systems, the goal is to help models and agents retrieve the right information at the right time.
- Good knowledge management reduces repeated searching, improves consistency, and makes answers easier to audit.
- It matters most when information must persist across sessions or change over time.
- If the knowledge base goes stale, AI behavior degrades with it.

## Consensus

- Knowledge management is the practice of creating, organizing, linking, sharing, and maintaining information so it can be found and reused effectively.
- In AI systems, it is more than file storage: it includes structure, taxonomy, summaries, backlinks, retrieval, and update routines.
- Its main value is making answers more accurate, consistent, auditable, and easier to reuse over time.
- It is especially relevant for assistants, service automation, research workflows, internal docs, and any system that needs persistent memory across sessions.

## Tensions / open questions

- The sources are aligned on the broad definition, but they differ slightly in emphasis: some frame knowledge management as business process and maintenance, while others frame it as an AI retrieval layer or a self-building wiki.
- The boundary between knowledge management and adjacent concepts like retrieval, documentation, and memory is fuzzy in these sources; they overlap heavily rather than being cleanly separated.
- The evidence does not compare alternative architectures or show when a lighter-weight approach is enough, so the practical threshold for 'how much KM is enough' remains open.

## Practical takeaway

Treat knowledge as a maintained system, not a static folder: structure it so AI can retrieve it, keep sources and summaries linked, and update it whenever the underlying business facts change.
