# Endava's AI-Native Delivery Rollout

## Implementation Study

### Overview

Endava embedded OpenAI tools into its software delivery and adjacent business workflows, then reorganized its process around an AI-native methodology called DavaFlow. The rollout extended beyond engineering into legal, project management, commercial planning, and leadership tasks.

### What was implemented?

ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex were made the enterprise AI platform, and DavaFlow was introduced as the delivery methodology spanning meeting prep, business planning, product discovery, software engineering, and deployment.

### Business objective

The company aimed to stay relevant in the AI era, speed delivery, and make AI part of everyday work rather than a separate initiative.

### Technical approach

OpenAI technology was embedded throughout the DavaFlow lifecycle, and employees used agents for summarization, inbox handling, asynchronous coordination, governance reports, and lightweight internal apps.

### Deployment context

The rollout was described across Endava's 11,000-person global workforce and included software delivery teams, legal, project management, commercial teams, and leadership groups.

### Outcome / current status

The article describes an ongoing internal transformation rather than a completed pilot; Endava says AI fluency is now part of hiring and promotion expectations.

### Why it succeeded or struggled

Adoption was reinforced by leadership usage, hands-on experimentation, and the decision to embed AI in everyday workflows instead of treating it as a separate tool rollout.

### Operational constraints

The source does not provide quantitative metrics, governance details, or failure analysis. It also does not specify the templates, controls, or security processes used to operate AI across a large workforce.

### AI / model observations

The case suggests that once agentic coding workflows land, upstream work such as requirements, planning, and coordination becomes a key bottleneck. It also suggests that AI value expands when models are used to generate operational artifacts, not only code.

### Implications for service automation

The article points to useful service-automation patterns in legal research, reporting, inbox management, and coordination, but it does not quantify service outcomes or containment effects.

### Strategic signals

The company is treating AI as part of its operating model, not merely a productivity add-on. The emphasis on orchestration suggests that integrated human-model workflows are becoming the main design problem.

### Key Lessons

- Embed AI into standard workflows rather than isolating it in a pilot.
- Expect bottlenecks to move from coding to planning, requirements, and coordination.
- Make leadership usage visible if you want broad adoption.
- Tie AI fluency to hiring and promotion if you want it to persist.

### Open Questions

- How much did delivery speed, quality, or cost actually improve?
- What governance and safety controls were used across the workforce?
- Which parts of DavaFlow were standardized versus left to team discretion?
