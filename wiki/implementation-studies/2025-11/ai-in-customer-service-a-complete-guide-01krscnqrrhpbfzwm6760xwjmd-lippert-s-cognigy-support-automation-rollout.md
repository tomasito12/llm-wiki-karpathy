# Lippert's Cognigy Support Automation Rollout

## Implementation Study

### Overview

Lippert deployed AI agents to handle product-related customer questions and reduce the burden on service teams dealing with detailed, repetitive support requests. The rollout focused on self-service for part pricing, availability, and order status.

### What was implemented?

AI agents for customer self-service, including responses to product-related queries and support for order-related tasks.

### Business objective

Reduce service load, improve self-service, and cut the cost of handling repetitive customer questions.

### Technical approach

The source says the company introduced AI agents from Cognigy to provide self-service for product-related queries.

### Deployment context

The deployment is described as a customer-service example for Lippert's support operations; the source does not specify pilot versus full production, but it presents operational results from the rollout.

### Outcome / current status

The source reports an 80% cost reduction from handled queries and a 37% containment rate.

### Why it succeeded or struggled

The apparent success came from choosing highly repetitive, information-heavy support tasks that fit self-service well. The source does not provide failure analysis.

### Operational constraints

Customer questions were specific and required specialized product knowledge, which made human handling expensive and slow.

### AI / model observations

This case suggests AI agents are strongest when the workflow has clear lookup and transaction patterns rather than open-ended problem solving.

### Implications for service automation

The example supports using AI self-service to absorb repetitive product-support demand and reduce human workload. It does not provide evidence about edge-case handling or long-tail exceptions.

### Strategic signals

This is a classic support-automation rollout: narrow scope, measurable containment, and direct cost impact. The source frames it as a practical customer-service system rather than a chatbot demo.

### Key Lessons

- Start with repetitive queries that have obvious resolution paths.
- Measure containment and cost impact rather than only user satisfaction.
- Specialized product support is a good fit for structured self-service.
- A narrow workflow can produce clearer operational gains than a broad assistant.

### Open Questions

- How stable were the reported savings over time?
- How much human oversight was still required for exceptions?
- What happened when customers asked about edge cases outside the workflow?
