# The Sequence Opinion #868: Recursion Is the New Scaling Law

This piece says AI may be entering a new phase. For years, the main way to get better models was to make them bigger and train them on more data. The article argues that the more interesting gains are coming from systems that loop: they search, revise, critique, and improve their own outputs. In plain terms, the article is about moving from one-shot answers to repeated self-correction. The big idea is that recursion, or doing useful work in loops, could matter more than just adding more parameters.

## Key insights

- The article treats recursive or loop-based computation as the candidate replacement for simple scale-up as the main driver of progress.
- It frames the key capability shift as models that can revisit and improve outputs, not just produce a single forward-pass answer.
- The argument is about agentic computing systems, where search, simulation, and critique are part of the computation loop.
- The excerpt provides a thesis, not evidence; it is useful as a framing lens rather than as a technical result.
- As of 2026-05-28, the idea is worth monitoring conceptually, but the source does not supply enough detail to adopt any specific recipe.

The piece is useful because it compresses a plausible direction for AI system design into one simple claim: repeated computation loops may become more important than raw model scale. For AI engineers, that is a reminder to evaluate systems by how well they can search, critique, and refine outputs, not only by first-pass answer quality. The article also helps explain why agentic systems attract attention: their value may come from structured iteration rather than a larger base model alone.
