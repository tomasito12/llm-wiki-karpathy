# Lippert's AI Agent Cuts Costs by 80% and Boosts Sales

This is a customer success story about using conversational AI for support. Lippert had many customer questions that needed detailed product knowledge, so it used Cognigy AI Agents to answer routine requests like pricing, availability, and order status. The company says this reduced support costs and helped more shoppers buy online. The interesting part is that the same system was used to improve both support efficiency and sales conversion. The article is useful as a practical example, but it is still a vendor case study, so the results should be treated as reported claims rather than independent proof.

## Key insights

- When support requests depend on product-specific knowledge, AI self-service can be aimed at a narrow set of high-volume questions first.
- The article frames business-user control as a deployment requirement: Lippert wanted internal teams to create and adjust agents without heavy engineering involvement.
- The reported value comes from two metrics at once: containment of support traffic and lower handling cost, not just a chatbot deployment.
- The case study links support automation to online conversion, suggesting that faster answers can affect purchase confidence, but the evidence is only the vendor's report.
- The stated roadmap extends from chat into voice, search, and agent assistance, which suggests a platform expansion pattern rather than a single-use bot.

## Why it matters

The piece is useful because it shows a concrete, product-level deployment pattern: start with a few information-heavy intents, use metadata and lexicons to support domain terminology, and let business users manage and extend the agents. It also gives a clear example of how a conversational AI vendor positions platform value beyond deflection, claiming both lower support cost and improved purchase conversion from faster, more accurate answers. For AI engineers, the durable takeaway is less about the headline numbers and more about the operational shape of the use case: a high-share of queries require structured product knowledge, so the value comes from surfacing reliable answers quickly rather than from open-ended generation. The article also hints that internal manageability matters as much as model capability, since the customer wanted a simple UI for expansion and adjustment. Because the evidence is a vendor-authored case study, the operational significance is real but thinly evidenced in this source. As of the article's publication date, it is best read as a practical implementation example to consider, not as proof that these results will generalize.

## Limitations / open questions

The article gives no implementation details on knowledge source quality, fallback behavior, human handoff, error rates, or how the 37% containment rate was measured. The 80% cost reduction is reported without a baseline definition, time period, or cost accounting method. It is unclear how much of the conversion lift can be attributed to the AI Agents versus other changes on the ecommerce site. The planned additions of Voice AI Agent, Knowledge AI search, and Agent Copilot are mentioned but not evaluated. Security, privacy, and governance concerns are not discussed.

## Contradictions / unverified claims

The strongest claims are promotional and come from a vendor case study, so they should be read as reported outcomes rather than independently verified benchmarks. The jump from handling support queries to increasing online sales conversion is plausible, but the article does not show causal analysis. The solution is presented as simple and scalable, yet the source does not show how much tuning or ongoing maintenance was needed to reach the reported results.
