# Things 3

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
A one-time-purchase task manager for Apple devices with Quick Entry and a simple Today/Upcoming/Anytime/Someday structure. The author prefers it over more complex or subscription-heavy task tools.

## Core Capabilities

- It supports fast global task capture through Quick Entry.
- It organizes tasks into a simple planning model that maps to the author’s thinking.
- It works as a one-time purchase rather than a subscription service.

## Integration Ecosystem

- It exists across Apple platforms, with iPhone and iPad sold separately from the Mac app.
- The author compares it with Todoist, TickTick, OmniFocus, and Apple Reminders as alternatives.

## Maturity signals

The app appears mature and stable enough that the author has cycled through multiple competitors and kept returning to it. The one-time purchase model and broad Apple-platform availability indicate a long-lived product with a conventional Mac software posture. The article does not claim broad enterprise adoption, but it does imply strong loyalty among solo Mac users.

## Strengths

- Quick Entry with Cmd+Option+Space is described as the fastest capture flow the author has found.
- The Today/Upcoming/Anytime/Someday structure matches how the author thinks about tasks, reducing mental translation overhead.
- One-time pricing is a real advantage in a software category that has shifted toward subscriptions.
- The interface is described as beautiful and fast, which matters because task managers are used constantly.

## Weaknesses / limitations

The source is clear about missing collaboration, time blocking, reliable location reminders, and Kanban views. Those omissions make it unsuitable for team coordination or more complex project-management workflows. The author also notes that heavier GTD practitioners may prefer OmniFocus.
