# Agent products sell infrastructure and adoption help, not just model access

## Interview Insight

### Summary

Both speakers argue that a background-agent product is not only the agent itself. Cognition describes selling compute handling, cloud deployment, onboarding, integrations, and help getting a company to actually adopt the workflow. Cole Murray says open-source background-agent systems sit in a hard middle layer where monetization is difficult because the model and sandbox layers capture obvious value, while the agent layer often looks like infrastructure and integrations.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-05-28, this is a useful product-model lens for agent tooling: the durable value is often in making the whole system operable inside enterprises. It also explains why point solutions that ignore onboarding, permissions, and integration depth may fail to convert into production use.

### Operational Relevance

For builders, this means planning for control planes, deployment support, integration engineering, and environment setup from the start rather than treating them as post-sale services. For buyers, it clarifies why a "deployed agent" still requires significant internal work to become useful.

### Service Automation Relevance

Directly relevant to service automation because the same integration burden appears in support and operations workflows: the agent must connect to logs, databases, Slack, and internal systems before it can help.
