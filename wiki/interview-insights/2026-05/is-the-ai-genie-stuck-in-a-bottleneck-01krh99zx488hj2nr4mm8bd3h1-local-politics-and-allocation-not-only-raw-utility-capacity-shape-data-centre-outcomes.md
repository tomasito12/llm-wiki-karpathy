# Local politics and allocation, not only raw utility capacity, shape data-centre outcomes

## Interview Insight

### Summary

The episode distinguishes between true grid limits and local friction around permits, transformer lead times, and pre-booked equipment. One guest argues that many announced load requests are speculative or phantom, while opposition groups and permitting delays are still very real. The practical lesson is that data-centre expansion is a coordination problem across utilities, communities, and financing, not a simple electricity-shortage story.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-05-07, infrastructure teams cannot treat power availability as a purely engineering question. Project viability depends on land, community acceptance, interconnection timing, equipment allocation, and whether financing is real rather than speculative.

### Operational Relevance

Build plans should separate committed capacity from paper demand, and should track permitting, transformer lead times, and utility queue quality. A realistic delivery model needs contingency paths for community opposition and for equipment that has been pre-purchased by projects that may never close.
