# Autonomy needs system-level economics, not just model quality

## Interview Insight

### Summary

The transcript repeatedly argues that drone performance depends on sensors, communications, motors, fiber optics, semiconductors, simulation, training, and manufacturing capacity, not just computer vision. The guest treats fiber optics as a communications tradeoff, not a universal fix, because it adds weight and cost even while improving resilience to jamming and radio-horizon limits. This is a broader infrastructure lesson: a capable model can still lose if the surrounding hardware stack is too expensive, fragile, or slow to scale.

### Why It Matters

Actionable as of 2026-05-18 because it reframes autonomy work as a full-stack deployment problem. For AI systems, this is a reminder that model improvement alone does not solve bottlenecks in latency, reliability, physical constraints, or supply chain dependence. The article’s strongest durable lesson is that scale and integration are part of capability, not an afterthought.

### Operational Relevance

Treat deployment as a coupled hardware-software problem: communications, sensing, compute, manufacturing, and training all constrain the final system. This is especially relevant when evaluating whether a model can survive adversarial interference or physical constraints. It also argues for cost-to-effect analysis, not just benchmark scores.
