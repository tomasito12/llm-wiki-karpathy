# AI expands demand for SaaS instead of replacing it

## Interview Insight

### Summary

Shipper argues that agent adoption will not kill SaaS; it will increase SaaS usage because agents create more users and more usage volume. He says companies should build for humans and agents together, and that token costs may shift toward users bringing their own AI rather than the SaaS vendor paying for every interaction. The practical result is a different product and margin model, not SaaS disappearance.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-05-24, this is a useful counterpoint to the "SaaS apocalypse" narrative. The operational takeaway is that vendors should prepare for agent traffic, agent-readable interfaces, and mixed human-agent consumption without assuming their product becomes obsolete. The claim is still a forecast, but it is grounded in a working example rather than a market study.

### Operational Relevance

SaaS teams should design APIs, HTML, and workflows so agents can use the product efficiently alongside humans. They should also think about cost allocation, since the user may supply the model tokens while the vendor supplies the software surface. This changes product economics, not just UX.

### Service Automation Relevance

For support and admin tooling, this implies that automation should drive more software interactions, not fewer. Service systems that are easy for agents to use may see higher request volume and should plan for that load.
