# Institutional friction can delay AI adoption even when the technology is ready

## Interview Insight

### Summary

Andreessen argues that the real economy is full of licensing, unions, public-sector protections, and other structures that slow change. He treats this as a reason why AI capability and AI adoption will not move in lockstep. The practical implication is that technical progress can be real while organizational uptake remains uneven.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-04-03, this is a useful caution against assuming that model capability automatically converts into enterprise transformation. AI engineering teams need deployment plans that account for regulation, procurement, labor rules, and institutional inertia. The source’s examples are concrete, but the claim is still a broad strategic view rather than a measured forecast.

### Operational Relevance

Expect uneven adoption across sectors. Build rollout strategies that separate technical feasibility from operational permission. In regulated or unionized environments, focus on new greenfield systems and adjacent workflows rather than expecting direct replacement of legacy processes.

### Service Automation Relevance

Support automation in particular will face policy, labor, and workflow constraints even when the technology works. This means pilots may succeed technically but stall at organizational adoption or governance review.
