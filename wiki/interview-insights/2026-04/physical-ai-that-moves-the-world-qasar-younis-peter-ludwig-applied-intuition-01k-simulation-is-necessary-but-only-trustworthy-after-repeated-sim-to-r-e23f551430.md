# Simulation is necessary but only trustworthy after repeated sim-to-real calibration

## Interview Insight

### Summary

The founders describe simulation as essential for development, but not sufficient on its own. They say no simulator perfectly represents the real world, so teams need repeated real-world feedback to tune the simulator until the gap is small enough to trust its outputs. They also argue that world models and neural simulation expand what can be simulated, but cannot eliminate physical testing.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-04-27, this is a durable evaluation lesson for autonomy and robotics teams: simulation should be treated as a calibrated development tool, not a substitute for deployment reality. It discourages overconfidence in simulator results and supports investment in measurement pipelines that connect lab performance to field behavior.

### Operational Relevance

Operationally, teams should define a sim-to-real validation loop, maintain real-world calibration data, and explicitly budget for field testing. Simulation environments should be updated when deployment conditions shift, rather than assumed correct by default.
