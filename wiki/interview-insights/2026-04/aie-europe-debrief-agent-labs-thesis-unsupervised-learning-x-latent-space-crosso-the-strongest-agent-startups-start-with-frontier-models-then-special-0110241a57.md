# The strongest agent startups start with frontier models, then specialize and distill

## Interview Insight

### Summary

The transcript describes an 'agent lab playbook' in which companies begin with frontier models, specialize them for a domain, and only later train or distill their own models once workload and high-quality user data justify the tradeoff. The argument is that custom models make sense when the company has enough repeated, high-volume work to save cost and latency. The same pattern is framed as a reversal of the usual bitter-lesson intuition: general models bootstrap the business first, then smaller specialized models take over.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-04-23, this is a practical go-to-market and architecture sequence for AI products that expect real usage. It helps founders decide when model training is a strategic lever versus premature optimization.

### Operational Relevance

Start with the best general model available, instrument the workflow, collect high-quality user traces, and only then consider distillation or in-house training for the stable parts of the workload. This reduces waste and keeps the training target tied to an actual operating profile.

### Service Automation Relevance

For service automation, this supports a staged approach: launch with frontier-model assistance, then specialize ticket handling, routing, or resolution patterns only after the support workload is well understood.
