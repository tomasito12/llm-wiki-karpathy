# Proof of human becomes more important than bot detection

## Interview Insight

### Summary

Andreessen argues that bot detection is no longer a reliable defense because models can pass the Turing test well enough to evade screening. His proposed answer is proof of human: biometric validation plus cryptographic proof, with selective disclosure so that identity checks do not reveal unnecessary personal data. He extends the same logic to proof of age and other attestations.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-04-03, this is a durable identity and abuse-prevention problem for AI products, platforms, and service automation. If bots can impersonate people convincingly, systems need positive identity proofs rather than heuristic bot scoring. The article is not a full solution, but it clearly identifies the direction of travel for authentication and privacy-preserving verification.

### Operational Relevance

Design identity systems around attestations, not just fraud detection. Separate proof of personhood, age, and other claims into selective-disclosure credentials. Assume adversarial accounts will be able to mimic human behavior and plan accordingly.

### Service Automation Relevance

For chatbots and support workflows, proof of human can help gate sensitive actions, reduce abuse, and manage account takeover risks. It also matters for escalation flows where an AI agent must verify who it is acting for.
