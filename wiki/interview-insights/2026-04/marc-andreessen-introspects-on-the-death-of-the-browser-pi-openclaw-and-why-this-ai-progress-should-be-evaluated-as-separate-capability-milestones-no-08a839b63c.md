# AI progress should be evaluated as separate capability milestones, not one monolithic model trend

## Interview Insight

### Summary

Andreessen frames the current wave as distinct breakthroughs in reasoning, coding, agents, and recursive self-improvement, rather than as a single scaling story. His point is that each step changes what systems can do in practice, and that practical usefulness matters more than whether the underlying architecture is fashionable. He treats the jump from chat to reasoning and then to coding as the moment AI stopped being easy to dismiss as pattern completion.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-04-03, this is a useful way to avoid overfitting on benchmark hype or on single-model releases. It encourages engineering teams to evaluate which capability boundary has actually moved, because product and workflow changes depend on whether the model can reason, code, use tools, or self-improve. That is a more durable mental model than treating every release as just another bigger chat model.

### Operational Relevance

Split model evaluation into separate gates for reasoning, code generation, agent execution, and self-improvement behaviors. Design adoption plans around the specific milestone that changed, not around generic quality scores. This supports better rollout decisions for coding agents, internal copilots, and tool-using workflows.

### Service Automation Relevance

Service automation systems should be judged by whether they can reliably reason over cases, take actions, and maintain state, not just answer questions. That distinction matters for escalation logic, tool access, and human handoff design.
