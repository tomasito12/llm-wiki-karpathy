# Pragmatic decentralized AI favors data, inference aggregation, and verification over distributed training

## Interview Insight

### Summary

Polosukhin says the pieces of decentralized AI that work today are practical ones: data collection, inference compute aggregation, verifiable benchmarks, and incentivized RL environments. He is skeptical that distributed training is the main value proposition because most users care about the best model, not where it was trained. The useful takeaway is that decentralized AI should be evaluated by whether it improves concrete infrastructure tasks, not by whether it sounds decentralized.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-04-02, this is a useful filter for separating durable infrastructure work from decentralization theater. It suggests that teams building in this space should optimize for measurable utility and verification rather than treating distributed training as the end goal.

### Operational Relevance

Prioritize infrastructure that improves data acquisition, aggregated inference capacity, benchmark verifiability, and rewardable training environments. Treat distributed model training as a specialized technique that must clear a high bar on performance and demand.

### Service Automation Relevance

Indirect but relevant: service systems can borrow the same logic by emphasizing measurable task completion, verification, and incentive design instead of celebrating distributed architecture for its own sake.
