# Knowledge Management

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
The practice of capturing, organizing, and retrieving information so people or systems can use it reliably later. In AI systems, it often includes structuring documents, policies, and historical interactions so models can answer with fewer errors and less repeated work.

## Related Terms

- Retrieval-Augmented Generation

## Relevance Note

Knowledge management is durable in AI systems because the quality of stored and retrievable operational knowledge often determines answer quality more than raw model capability. It is especially important in chatbots, service agents, and internal assistants that need stable, auditable responses across many customer interactions.

### The ultimate guide to knowledge management for your Sales Agent (2026-05-13)

- In practice, knowledge management is less about storing files and more about making sure the right information can be found, understood, and updated when it matters. For AI systems, that means the content must be structured in a way that models and agents can retrieve and use without guessing. It also has a maintenance side: if pricing changes, product features shift, or qualification rules evolve, the knowledge base has to change with them. Teams that treat knowledge as a living system generally get more reliable agent behavior than teams that treat it as a static library.
