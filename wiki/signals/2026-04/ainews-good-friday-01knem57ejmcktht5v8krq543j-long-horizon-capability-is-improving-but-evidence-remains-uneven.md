# Long-horizon capability is improving, but evidence remains uneven

## Signal

### Summary

The roundup cites METR-style time-horizon results showing capability gains over time and notes models reaching tasks that take human experts hours. It also points to recursive context management and self-distillation as active research paths. The signal is directionally positive, but the source presents these results as partial and still method-dependent.

### Why It Matters

Long-horizon work is central to autonomous agents and complex workflows, so even modest progress has architectural implications. But practitioners should avoid over-reading a single methodology or extrapolated timeline.

### Operational Relevance

Evaluation teams should separate headline time-horizon numbers from actual deployment readiness. Better context management and post-training methods may matter more than raw capability claims for production agents.

### Service Automation Relevance

Longer-horizon progress could improve multi-step support cases, but only if the system also preserves state and avoids drift across turns.
