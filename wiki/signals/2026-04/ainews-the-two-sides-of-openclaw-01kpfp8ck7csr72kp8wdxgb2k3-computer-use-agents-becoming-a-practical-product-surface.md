# Computer-use agents becoming a practical product surface

## Signal

### Summary

Practitioner reactions in the roundup suggest that computer-use systems are moving from novelty to usable workflow tools. The comments focus on speed and the ability to drive Slack, browser flows, and desktop applications, which matters for legacy enterprise software.

### Why It Matters

If this signal holds, the bottleneck shifts from “can a model click around a screen?” to “can the harness make it reliable enough for real work.” That has implications for orchestration, logging, human handoff, and approval flows.

### Operational Relevance

Teams building agents may need to invest more in desktop-state handling, guardrails, and task decomposition than in raw prompting. The article’s comments imply that simple wrappers around desktop apps can become a serious integration path.

### Service Automation Relevance

Potentially relevant for support and back-office workflows that still depend on legacy desktop or browser tools, but the source does not provide deployment evidence for those use cases.
