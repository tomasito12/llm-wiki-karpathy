# Open agent progress is shifting toward trace capture and curation

## Signal

### Summary

The roundup argues that open-source frontier agents need more than harnesses and prompts; they need usable trace data from real sessions. Tools like pi-share-hf and the Signals paper point to publishing, filtering, and learning from coding-agent traces as an emerging stack. The important operational move is turning production usage into training or evaluation data with privacy controls.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-04-07, this is one of the most durable technical signals in the piece because it connects data collection, evaluation, and training for agents. If accepted broadly, it changes how teams think about building agent improvements: not from synthetic demos, but from logged real work with governance around reuse.

### Operational Relevance

Teams building agents should plan for trace logging, consent/PII handling, session publishing, and curation workflows. The source suggests that real production traces may become the highest-value asset for agent tuning and evaluation.

### Service Automation Relevance

Directly relevant to support automation because chat and workflow traces can be converted into evaluation and training data. The article does not prove the full governance pipeline, so deployment teams should treat this as promising but incomplete.
