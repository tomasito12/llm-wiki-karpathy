# Restricted frontier access for cyber-capable models

## Signal

### Summary

Anthropic’s Claude Mythos Preview is described as a model that is not being broadly released because of cyber risk, with access limited to 40 partners under Project Glasswing. The operational implication is that model distribution can be constrained when capability crosses a safety threshold. This is a direct signal that release strategy may diverge from capability strategy.

### Why It Matters

If this pattern repeats, frontier vendors may increasingly separate 'best model' from 'widely available model' when safety risk is high. That would affect procurement, benchmarking, and product planning because access rights may matter as much as raw model quality.

### Operational Relevance

Teams building on frontier models may need approval workflows, partner-tier access, and auditable controls for the most capable systems. It also suggests that benchmarking alone may not tell you whether a capability is deployable in production.

### Service Automation Relevance

No direct service automation implications identified.

### Evidence Snippets

- Anthropic officially unveiled Claude Mythos Preview and Project Glasswing, a restricted-access cyberdefense initiative rather than a public API launch.
- Anthropic said Mythos can find software vulnerabilities better than all but the most skilled humans and is being provided to a coalition to secure critical software instead of being generally released.
