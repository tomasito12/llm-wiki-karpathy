# Enterprise AI moat shifts from packaged applications to trust layers

## Signal

### Summary

The article argues that enterprise software middlemen do not simply disappear as agents improve; their role shifts toward governance, permissions, identity, memory, and auditability. The durable value is described as the layer where intention becomes permitted action. That is relevant for system design, product positioning, and platform boundaries.

### Why It Matters

If this framing holds as of 2026-06-03, vendors that only bolt AI onto existing products may struggle to defend their role. Teams building enterprise agents may need to treat trust and permission handling as core product surfaces rather than add-ons.

### Operational Relevance

Design around governed action, not just model access. Expect product work on permissions, provenance, and audit trails to matter as much as prompt quality or model choice.

### Service Automation Relevance

Service automation systems will need reliable handoff logic, approval boundaries, and traceability before they can safely execute on behalf of users at scale.

### Evidence Snippets

- What stays expensive is trust: knowing an agent acted on the right data, with the right permissions, on behalf of the right person, with a record of why.
- The middleman doesn't disappear in the direct-use world; it changes shape.
- It stops being an application ... and becomes a substrate, the trust and permission layer that sits at the moment a stated intention turns into a permitted action.
