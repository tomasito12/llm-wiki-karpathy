# Realtime voice agents are becoming workflow-completion systems

## Signal

### Summary

OpenAI’s GPT-Realtime-2 is presented as a voice model for agents that can reason mid-conversation, call tools, handle interruptions, and recover from repairs. The article’s practical takeaway is that voice apps are shifting from speech wrappers toward live systems that complete tasks while keeping the conversation flowing. The source also points to real integrations at Glean, Genspark, and Vimeo, which suggests this is being tested in production workflows rather than only demos.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-05-08, this is operationally important because voice quality is no longer just an ASR/TTS problem; latency, turn-taking, tool transparency, and recovery behavior become part of the product surface. That changes how builders design assistants for support, meetings, translation, and other hands-free workflows.

### Operational Relevance

Designing voice agents as stateful real-time systems, with harnesses for interruptions, tool calls, and long-session memory, becomes more important than prompt-only chat design.

### Service Automation Relevance

Strong relevance: customer support and call-handling systems can benefit from audible tool calls, interruption recovery, and longer conversational continuity, especially in live voice workflows.

### Evidence Snippets

- "The technical shift is not just better ASR or TTS; it is the combination of low-latency turn-taking, interruption handling, longer context, tool-call transparency, and adjustable reasoning effort in a single real-time loop."
- "GPT-Realtime-2 is framed as a real-time agent model, not just a speech front end: it adds tool use, interruption recovery, longer context, and controllable preambles."
- "The model can call multiple tools at once and make those actions audible with phrases like 'checking your calendar' or 'looking that up now,' helping agents stay responsive while completing tasks."
