# LLMs are becoming a practical force multiplier for cyber offense and defense

## Signal

### Summary

The Google report summarized in the roundup describes several attack patterns made easier by LLMs: malware mutation, logical-flaw discovery, traffic obfuscation, and attacks on AI infrastructure itself. The article frames this as a widening gap between model-assisted attack speed and traditional defensive workflows. The operational implication is that defenders need proactive review and patching practices that assume attackers can iterate faster.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-05-22, the durable takeaway is not a generic security warning but the specific shift toward LLM-assisted exploit discovery and scaling. That changes how security teams should prioritize code review, patch response, and infrastructure hardening.

### Operational Relevance

AI engineering teams should assume that model-assisted vulnerability finding and attack automation can reduce attacker effort. This increases the importance of secure-by-design harnesses, prompt and tool boundary hardening, and reviewing AI infrastructure as an attack surface.

### Service Automation Relevance

Indirect but important: any service automation stack that exposes tools, credentials, or admin surfaces through agents expands the attack surface and needs tighter controls.
