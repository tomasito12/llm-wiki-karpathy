# AI-driven web traffic is shifting toward agentic browsing and transaction-like behavior

## Signal

### Summary

Human Security reports that AI-driven traffic nearly tripled in 2025, with a small but fast-growing share coming from agents and agentic browsers. The notable operational change is not just scale, but that these systems browse products, create accounts, and complete transactions in ways that resemble human users. That makes bot detection and infrastructure planning harder.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-05-30, websites can no longer assume that browsing, login, and checkout patterns cleanly distinguish people from bots. Defenses and capacity planning need to account for legitimate AI agents and abusive automation using similar interaction patterns.

### Operational Relevance

Important for abuse prevention, rate limiting, identity checks, and load management on web-facing systems. It also suggests that telemetry and fraud models must distinguish intent, not just interaction shape.

### Service Automation Relevance

Indirectly relevant: agentic commerce and support flows may increase automated traffic to account, authentication, and transaction pages, which can affect chatbot-linked workflows and customer-service systems.

### Evidence Snippets

- AI-driven traffic, or internet interactions that were generated by or on behalf of AI systems, nearly tripled in 2025, according to a report by the cybersecurity firm Human Security.
- Traffic by AI agents and agentic browsers ballooned (although it remained a tiny percentage of the total).
- Of the agentic interactions, 77 percent took place on product and search pages.
- The rise in AI-driven traffic also poses challenges for cybersecurity because legitimate AI agents perform many of the same activities — browsing products, creating accounts, and checking out of transactions — that previously signaled malicious bots.
