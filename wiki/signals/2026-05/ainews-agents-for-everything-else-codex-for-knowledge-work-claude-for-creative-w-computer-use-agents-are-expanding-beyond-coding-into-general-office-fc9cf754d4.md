# Computer-use agents are expanding beyond coding into general office work

## Signal

### Summary

The Codex update is explicitly positioned for non-coding computer work, with onboarding into Microsoft, Google, and Salesforce, plus support for docs, slides, spreadsheets, research, and planning. This suggests agent UX is being organized around task completion across common business software rather than around code-only workflows. The practical implication is that evaluation, permissions, and UI routing matter as much as raw model quality.

### Why It Matters

As of 2026-05-01, this is one of the clearest examples of a coding agent being repackaged as a broader computer-use system. The source is strong on product signals but thin on failure modes, so the main durable lesson is about workflow packaging, not a proof of general capability.

### Operational Relevance

Teams building agents should expect higher value from app connectors, task-specific UI, and file-native interactions than from chat-only interfaces. The source also implies that onboarding and handoff design are part of the agent product surface.

### Service Automation Relevance

Potentially relevant for back-office automation and assisted operations, but the source does not provide evidence for customer support outcomes specifically. It mainly supports office-work and document-processing use cases.
