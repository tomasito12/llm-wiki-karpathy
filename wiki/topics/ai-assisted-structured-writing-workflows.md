# AI-Assisted Structured Writing Workflows

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
AI-assisted writing workflows combine freeform drafting with post-processing steps that turn text into more usable artifacts. A common pattern is to keep the human in the writing loop while delegating interruption handling, lookup, and extraction tasks to the model. The main operational value is not writing replacement but reducing friction between thinking and producing an actionable output. These workflows are strongest when they preserve flow, minimize context switching, and convert unstructured notes into downstream work products.

## Examples

The source describes writing morning pages in an AI-enabled editor, asking the assistant questions inline, and then having it "pull out anything that sounds like a todo."

## Key Points

- Inline assistance reduces the need to open separate tabs and interrupt flow.
- Post-hoc extraction turns unstructured writing into actionable tasks.
- The workflow is more reusable than the specific morning-pages ritual because it is about structured drafting plus downstream conversion.

## Operational Insight

Treat AI as a drafting companion and structuring layer, not just a generation engine. The durable design choice is to let the human preserve flow while the model handles interruptions and post-hoc extraction.
