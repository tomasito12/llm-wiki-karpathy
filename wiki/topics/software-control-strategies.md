# Software Control Strategies

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
Software control strategies are business and product approaches that move beyond selling tools and toward controlling customer workflows, distribution, data access, pricing, and industry behavior. Common forms include subscription gating, platform dependency, centralized policy control, and replacing customer-owned processes with vendor-managed systems. The durable engineering relevance is that control changes product design: systems are optimized for lock-in, routing, and governance rather than only user productivity. This pattern is especially important in AI products because model access, orchestration, and data handling can become leverage points over entire workflows.

## Key Points

- Control strategies center on who governs the workflow, not only on feature performance.
- Subscriptions and platform dependencies can be used to shift power from customers to vendors.
- AI products can intensify this pattern because model access and orchestration sit in the middle of many business processes.
- The strategic question is whether a product improves user autonomy or narrows it.

## Operational Insight

When a product’s value proposition shifts from helping users do work to controlling the terms on which work can happen, the architecture, pricing, and policy layer become part of the core product, not just support functions.
