# AI Program Ownership

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
When an AI system is embedded in a business motion, it should be treated as its own operational source rather than folded invisibly into existing team metrics. The source argues for dedicated program ownership so someone is accountable for strategy, implementation, and ongoing optimization, instead of leaving AI as a bolt-on to a role. It also shows that ownership should cover measurement, targets, and workflow integration so the AI motion can be improved continuously and judged against clear outcomes.

## Key Points

- Dedicated ownership helps separate the AI motion from the surrounding human team.
- Program leads should own strategy, implementation, and optimization rather than just tool administration.
- A continuous optimization loop matters more than a one-time setup when the AI sits in a live workflow.
- This kind of ownership mirrors how service automation programs are often run when they scale.

## Operational Insight

Assign one accountable owner for the AI motion, with responsibility for performance, rollout, and iterative optimization; do not assume a general manager or existing team will manage it incidentally.
