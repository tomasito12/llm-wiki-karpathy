# Governance as an Enabler for Enterprise AI

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
Enterprise AI scales more reliably when governance is treated as part of the design process rather than as a late approval gate. Security, legal, compliance, and IT can reduce rework if they participate early in shaping workflows, controls, and launch criteria. The practical goal is not maximum restriction; it is faster movement with fewer reversals because the system is trusted by the people who must approve and operate it. This pattern matters most in regulated or process-heavy environments where rollout friction, not model capability, becomes the limiting factor.

## Examples

Interviews with executives at Philips, BBVA, Mirakl, Scout24, Jetbrains and Scania emphasized that "Where security, legal, compliance, and IT were involved early as design partners, teams moved faster later—with fewer reversals and more trust."

## Key Points

- Early involvement from security, legal, compliance, and IT can reduce reversals later.
- Governance can speed delivery when it is built into design instead of added after implementation.
- This is most relevant where trust and approval are necessary for production rollout.
