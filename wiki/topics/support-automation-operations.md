# Support Automation Operations

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
Support automation works best when the support function is redesigned around the automation layer, not merely augmented with a chatbot. In the source example, that redesign included a dedicated AI Support team, a Knowledge Manager role, a Conversation Designer role, and expanded Support Operations coverage for optimization work such as enablement, workforce management, QA, process management, and data insights. The organization also restructured human support into new job families as AI took over more routine volume, while resetting expectations so time is explicitly split between customer support and improving AI.

## Key Points

- A dedicated AI Support team can own ongoing optimization and adoption strategy.
- Knowledge work should be formalized into a role and embedded into product launch processes.
- Conversation design should cover both automated interactions and human handoff experiences.
- Support Operations can expand to include AI-driven optimization across enablement, WFM, QA, process management, and data insights.
- Human support roles may need new job families as the mix of work becomes more complex.
- Teams must explicitly reserve time to improve the automation system, not only answer tickets.

## Operational Insight

Treat support automation as an operating-model change: define explicit ownership for AI optimization, knowledge quality, conversation flow, and support analytics, and budget protected time for continuous improvement as the automated system expands.
