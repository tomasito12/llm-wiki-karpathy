# Organizational AI Readiness

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
Organizational AI readiness is the degree to which a company’s content, boundaries, processes, data, and accountability are prepared for an AI system to take useful actions. It is not just a model capability issue; it depends on whether the surrounding business and technical systems can support safe execution. In practice, readiness determines whether an agent can only answer and guide, or can also look things up, make changes, and resolve multi-step work. The concept is useful because many agent failures come from missing operational structure rather than weak model performance.

## Key Points

- Readiness can be decomposed into content, scope, procedural, data, and execution layers.
- The first two layers are common; the last three usually create the real bottlenecks.
- Readiness is partly technical, but execution readiness also requires ownership and recovery paths.
- Teams may need to redesign agents into guiding modes when action is not yet safe or feasible.
- Leadership participation matters; sponsorship alone is not enough.
- Governance works best when security, legal, compliance, and IT are involved in design, not only at review time.
- Workflow fit and trust mechanisms are prerequisites for adoption at scale.
- Quality criteria should be defined before launch to avoid rework and reversals.

## Operational Insight

Evaluate agent projects by the weakest readiness layer, not by raw model capability alone. A system can be strong on conversation quality and still be blocked by undocumented procedures, unreliable data, or unclear ownership for mistakes.

In practice, readiness shows up in whether teams can move from experimentation to repeatable operational use.
