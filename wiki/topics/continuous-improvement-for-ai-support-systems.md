# Continuous Improvement for AI Support Systems

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
AI support systems improve more reliably when changes are treated as a repeating operational loop rather than a one-time launch. The loop usually includes identifying a small failure mode, testing a change, measuring the result, and refining the system. This approach works best when both automated and human workflows feed lessons back into the product. It is especially useful in service automation, where edge cases and support interactions expose weaknesses that are hard to see in isolated tests.

## Examples

A concrete illustration is the four-step loop described for Agent Fin: "Train: Teach Fin how to handle and resolve the most complex customer queries. Test: Run fully simulated customer conversations from start to finish... Deploy... Analyze."

## Key Points

- Small, repeated changes are easier to adopt than large periodic rewrites.
- Simulation before deployment can catch failure modes without exposing customers to them.
- Human support interactions can be a structured source of product improvement.
- Low-friction suggestion capture matters because improvement systems fail when logging is burdensome.

## Operational Insight

Treat support quality as an operating system with recurring feedback, not as a static model quality problem. The practical leverage comes from making training, simulation, deployment, and analysis part of one loop.
