# Voice Prompting for Conversational Systems

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
Voice systems need prompts that shape spoken delivery, not just textual content. In practice, prompt quality depends on how the assistant should sound, where it should pause, how long it should speak, and how polite or formal it should be in a specific conversational setting. This makes voice prompting closer to speech direction than ordinary chat prompting. The operational challenge is to coordinate intent, script, pacing, and cultural tone at the same time.

## Key Points

- Voice prompting targets script and intent rather than directly commanding the voice output layer.
- Punctuation can change perceived speech rhythm, pauses, and emphasis.
- Local business norms can change what counts as a friendly or respectful tone.
- Concise responses matter because spoken interactions are less tolerant of verbosity and delay.

## Operational Insight

Treat the written response as a performance script. Use wording, punctuation, and brevity to steer pacing and delivery, but assume the speech layer may still impose its own defaults.
