# Tax Base Shift From Labor To Capital

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
If wage income shrinks as a share of total income, tax systems that depend heavily on labor can become less reliable. Governments then have to replace payroll- and income-based revenue with taxes that reach consumption, capital returns, or rents. The key tradeoff is that broad taxes are usually more stable and less distortionary, while targeted taxes may be easier to sell politically but can distort investment and spending. This becomes especially important when public spending needs rise at the same time the labor tax base weakens.

## Key Points

- The OECD average still raises about half of tax revenue from labor.
- Consumption taxes are presented as the most obvious replacement for lost labor-tax revenue.
- Capital and rent taxation can capture value when AI shifts income toward asset owners.
- Flashy narrow levies like robot taxes and token taxes fit inside standard tax categories but likely distort behavior.

## Operational Insight

The durable planning lesson is to model fiscal exposure as an input to policy design: if labor income is the dominant tax base, automation risk becomes revenue risk.
