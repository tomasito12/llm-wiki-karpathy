# State Control Over AI Supply Chains

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
Government policy can shape AI development through compute access, chip procurement, merger approvals, and mobility restrictions on founders and key staff. Those constraints affect not just training cost but also timing, infrastructure choice, and organizational freedom. In some markets, the practical limits on AI progress come from procurement and regulation as much as from model science. Practitioners should treat infrastructure availability and policy exposure as first-order deployment variables.

## Key Points

- Compute strategy is exposed to policy decisions when governments push specific chip suppliers or restrict acquisitions.
- Infrastructure choice can change training cost and schedule even when model architecture is unchanged.
- Founders and ownership controls can become practical constraints on AI company autonomy.

## Operational Insight

Plan model and infrastructure strategy around the possibility that chip supply, vendor access, or corporate actions can be constrained by policy. If a region depends on state-favored hardware or approvals, timing and cost assumptions can change outside the engineering team’s control.
