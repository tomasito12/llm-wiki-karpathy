# AI Model Differentiation Shifts to Distribution

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
When model quality becomes good enough across several providers, the competitive edge can move toward where the model is embedded, how it is packaged, and which workflows it enables. A model release that once would have stood out on benchmarks can feel routine if rivals have closed the gap. Product layers, merchant workflows, and app ecosystems can become more important than raw model announcements. The durable lesson is that model capability and distribution should be evaluated together, not separately.

## Key Points

- A model can remain competitive while still failing to create market excitement if the surrounding ecosystem has matured.
- Distribution and workflow fit can matter more than a marginal model improvement once several players are close in capability.
- Model launches should be evaluated with the product layer in mind, especially when the source says attention has moved to apps built on top of AI.

## Operational Insight

Treat benchmark parity as the start of product competition, not the finish. If a model is no longer obviously ahead on cost or capability, the next moat is usually integration, workflow attachment, and customer-facing utility.
