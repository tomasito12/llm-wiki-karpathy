# AI Workflows Shift Toward Supervised Friction

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
AI-assisted workflows increasingly need explicit friction, review gates, and stopping rules to prevent easy generation from overwhelming attention and maintenance capacity. The core design problem is shifting from making output easier to making sure output is worth keeping.

## Related Trends

- workflow-restructuring-around-ai-agents

## Supporting Data Points

- The author describes many AI-built projects that were deleted, abandoned, or never completed.
- The author says reducing subscription limits did not stop overuse because faster tools encouraged more usage.
- A failed speech-to-blog pipeline is used as an example of how removing effort also removed commitment.

## Time sensitivity

Actionable as of 2026-05-31; relevant while AI tools still optimize for low-friction, high-volume interaction.

## Uncertainty / maturity

The evidence is anecdotal and self-reported, so it supports a plausible workflow pattern rather than a measured industry-wide shift. Different tasks may benefit from lower friction, so the right balance is context-dependent.
