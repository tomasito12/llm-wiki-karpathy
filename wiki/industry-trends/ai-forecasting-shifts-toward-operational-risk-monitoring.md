# AI Forecasting Shifts Toward Operational Risk Monitoring

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
AI forecasting systems are moving from abstract prediction exercises toward operational monitoring of concrete risks such as unrest, displacement, and escalation. The emphasis is on feeding models richer streams of historical and real-time indicators so they can support decision-making in live operational settings. This trend is strongest where organizations can act on the forecast, such as defense, foreign policy, and humanitarian response. It does not imply that forecasting is solved; it implies that the use case is becoming more operationally valuable despite uncertainty.

## Supporting Data Points

- RAND's ISF estimated a 20% chance of regime collapse or replacement in Iran by the end of 2026.
- ACLED's CAST is used by UN agencies and the Dutch foreign ministry.
- VIEWS has been adopted by the UN Development Programme and the EU's diplomatic service.
- The IOM forecasted that 304,362 people in Somalia would be displaced over three months.

## Time sensitivity

Actionable as of 2026-05-13; the operationalization described here reflects a live adoption pattern as of the publication date, but the durability of any one system's performance remains uncertain.

## Uncertainty / maturity

The evidence is mixed: some systems are already in use, but the article also says predictions of new conflict onset remain unreliable and that several users only find the models useful once strife is already under way.
