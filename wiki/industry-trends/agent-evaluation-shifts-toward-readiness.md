# Agent Evaluation Shifts Toward Organizational Readiness

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
Agent evaluation is moving from capability-only testing toward assessing whether an organization can safely permit the agent to act. The relevant question is not only whether the model can perform a task, but whether the company has clear boundaries, stable data access, documented procedures, and accountable recovery paths. This changes evaluation from a product benchmark mindset to a production-readiness mindset.

## Supporting Data Points

- The article says the observed teams were constrained by what the organization was structured to allow.
- It identifies five readiness layers: content, scope, procedural, data, and execution.
- Across six industries, teams often moved to guiding workflows when infrastructure or governance was missing.

## Time sensitivity

Actionable as of 2026-05-18; the shift matters whenever teams are trying to expand agent permissions beyond simple answering and routing.

## Uncertainty / maturity

The evidence is observational and framed by one author’s cross-industry study, so it is directionally useful but not a quantified general law. Model capability can still be the limiting factor in some deployments, so readiness is not the only constraint.
