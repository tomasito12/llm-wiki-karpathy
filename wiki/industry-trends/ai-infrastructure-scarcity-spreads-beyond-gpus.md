# AI Infrastructure Scarcity Spreads Beyond GPUs

## Current understanding

<!-- stage1-placeholder: single-source lead; Stage 2 will synthesize from accumulated EvidenceItems -->
AI scaling pressure can move through the physical stack rather than staying confined to one component. As demand rises, shortages can appear in GPUs, memory, CPUs, networking gear, cooling equipment, data-center construction, and fabrication capacity, forcing operators to manage access and product scope around supply constraints. The operational implication is that bottlenecks in AI systems are increasingly system-wide and cross-vendor, not isolated to model inference hardware.

## Supporting Data Points

- OpenRouter weekly tokens processed quadrupled between January and March.
- Anthropic began throttling access at busy times and had around 30 minutes a day of outages in April.
- H100 rental prices have risen by around 30% since November.
- All three big HBM producers say most of their 2026 supply is sold out.
- TSMC plans to spend about $55bn in 2026, up 34% from 2025, while the five hyperscalers have tripled combined capital spending to more than $750bn.

## Time sensitivity

Actionable as of 2026-04-27; the source argues the squeeze is already present and could persist while new capacity takes years to build.

## Uncertainty / maturity

The article is a reported snapshot, not a full audited capacity model, so the exact duration and severity of each shortage remain uncertain. Some relief is already visible in the text through workload shifts, older-generation chips, and memory-saving software, so the bottleneck may ease unevenly.
